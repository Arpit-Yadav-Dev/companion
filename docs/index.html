A little reminder that walks onto your screen

Companion nudges you to drink water, stretch, and step away — with a character that walks on, asks, and walks off. Nothing to read. Nothing to dismiss.

This is the whole app. It appears, asks, and leaves.

Why it works

Notifications pile up in a corner and get swiped away without reading. A character walking across your screen is impossible to ignore — and takes one click to answer.

Stays out of your way

You can click straight through it. Only the character and its two buttons catch the cursor, so it never blocks what you're doing.

Knows when you're away

Pauses while you're gone, and won't greet you with a pile of missed reminders when you come back or wake your Mac.

Yours alone

No account, no sign-up, no tracking. Everything stays on your Mac.

Eleven reminders, or write your own

Water, stretch, eye rest, posture, walk, medicine, lunch, breathe, wrap up, time to leave, wind down. Each one has its own schedule, wording and buttons — and only water is on until you say otherwise.

Thirty-three characters

Fourteen are drawn into the app and work the moment you install it. Nineteen more are animated from video and download from inside Settings when you pick one — which is why this is a 7 MB download and not a 75 MB one.

Installing it

  1. Download the file Companion_0.1.0_universal.dmg — one file, 7 MB, works on every Mac from 2017 on.
  2. Drag Companion into Applications Open the downloaded file and drag the icon across, the same as any Mac app.
  3. Right-click it and choose Open Right-click (or Control-click) Companion in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. Just the first time.
  4. Look up at your menu bar A small face appears near the clock. Click it for Settings — there's no Dock icon and no window, that's normal.
  1. Download the installer Companion_0.1.0_x64-setup.exe — 3 MB, for Windows 10 and 11.
  2. Run it Double-click the downloaded file and follow the prompts.
  3. Click "More info", then "Run anyway" Windows SmartScreen warns about installers it hasn't seen before. The warning hides the Run button behind More info.
  4. Look in your system tray A small face appears near the clock, bottom-right. You may need to click the ^ arrow to see hidden icons.
.exe or .msi — which one? Take the .exe. It's the normal installer and what the button above gives you. The .msi is the same app in the format IT departments use to deploy software across many machines at once — useful if that's your situation, unnecessary otherwise.
Brand new on Windows The Windows build was compiled but has never been run on a Windows machine. It may well have rough edges the Mac version doesn't — tell me what breaks.
Why step 3? Opening it by double-clicking shows "cannot be opened because the developer cannot be verified" — macOS says that about any app not signed with a paid Apple certificate, which this one isn't. Right-click → Open is the standard way past it, and you only do it once.
